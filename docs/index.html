The method

A rent increase is a bet, not a decision. Every raise carries two outcomes, and most landlords only price one of them — the extra income. The other one, a turnover, is bigger than it feels: vacancy loss plus make-ready cost, plus the quiet risk that the next tenant pays late.

Expected value = (1 − p) × annual raise − p × turnover cost

where p is your honest chance the tenant walks. A $75 raise is $900 a year. A single 30-day turnover on an $1,875 unit plus $2,200 of make-ready is over $4,000. The raise has to win roughly 4 out of 5 coin flips just to break even. That's the asymmetry: small gains, occasionally large losses. Run it, respect it.

A known good tenant is an asset. Price the raise like you'd price losing one.
